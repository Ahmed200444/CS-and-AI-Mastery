01Generator and discriminator

What you will learn

  • Explain the two networks a GAN trains and their opposing goals
  • Explain how generator changes the behavior, result, or engineering decision in a concrete example
  • Connect generator with discriminator and predict what changes when one assumption changes
  • Verify the idea with a normal case and an important edge, failure, or scale case

Explanation

A GAN trains two networks against each other: the Generator takes random noise and tries to produce realistic fake data (e.g. images); the Discriminator looks at real and fake data and tries to correctly tell them apart.

Key concepts

generatordiscriminatoradversarial pair

Example

noise = random_vector()
fake_image = generator(noise)
is_real_prediction = discriminator(fake_image)  # discriminator tries to catch it
# generator wants discriminator to say 'real'; discriminator wants to say 'fake'
Common mistake: Thinking of the discriminator as the 'important' network and the generator as secondary — the generator is actually the model you keep and deploy; the discriminator's whole job is to make the generator better, then it's typically discarded.
02Adversarial training

What you will learn

  • Describe the alternating training process between generator and discriminator
  • Explain how alternating updates changes the behavior, result, or engineering decision in a concrete example
  • Connect alternating updates with competitive improvement and predict what changes when one assumption changes
  • Verify the idea with a normal case and an important edge, failure, or scale case

Explanation

Training alternates: first the discriminator is updated to get better at spotting the generator's current fakes, then the generator is updated to get better at fooling the discriminator's current judgment. Each network's improvement pushes the other to improve too — ideally converging toward a generator producing genuinely realistic outputs.

Key concepts

alternating updatescompetitive improvement

Example

for step in range(num_steps):
    train_discriminator_one_step(real_batch, fake_batch)
    train_generator_one_step(discriminator)  # generator learns from discriminator's feedback
Common mistake: Training one network to convergence before touching the other — GANs need roughly BALANCED progress; letting one network dominate is a primary cause of training instability.
03The minimax objective

What you will learn

  • Explain the minimax framing of GAN training at a conceptual level
  • Explain how minimax changes the behavior, result, or engineering decision in a concrete example
  • Connect minimax with adversarial objective and predict what changes when one assumption changes
  • Verify the idea with a normal case and an important edge, failure, or scale case

Explanation

GAN training is framed as a minimax game: the discriminator tries to MAXIMIZE its ability to correctly classify real vs fake; the generator tries to MINIMIZE the discriminator's success (i.e., maximize how often it's fooled).

Key concepts

minimaxadversarial objectiveno single loss to just minimize

Example

# conceptually:
# discriminator wants to MAXIMIZE: correctly labeling real as real, fake as fake
# generator wants to MINIMIZE: the discriminator's success rate on its fakes
# -- two opposing objectives trained simultaneously
Common mistake: Expecting GAN training loss curves to behave like a normal supervised model's steadily-decreasing loss — GAN losses can oscillate even in a HEALTHY training run, which is confusing if you're expecting standard behavior.
04Training instability & mode collapse

What you will learn

  • Diagnose the two most common GAN training failures
  • Explain how training instability changes the behavior, result, or engineering decision in a concrete example
  • Connect training instability with mode collapse and predict what changes when one assumption changes
  • Verify the idea with a normal case and an important edge, failure, or scale case

Explanation

GAN training can be unstable because the generator and discriminator learn against each other, so one can overpower the other. Mode collapse happens when the generator produces only a few kinds of outputs instead of the full data variety. You learn to spot both problems from losses and samples, then judge whether a training change improves quality and diversity.

Key concepts

training instabilitymode collapseloss oscillation

Example

# mode collapse symptom: generate 20 samples, get 20 near-identical outputs
# despite the training data having huge diversity
# -- the generator found a 'cheat' that fools the discriminator, and stopped exploring
Common mistake: Assuming a decreasing generator loss means training is going well — a generator can achieve low loss by cheaply fooling the discriminator with the SAME few outputs (mode collapse) rather.

06Conditional GANs & image-to-image generation

What you will learn

  • Explain how conditioning extends a GAN beyond unconstrained generation
  • Explain how conditional GAN changes the behavior, result, or engineering decision in a concrete example
  • Connect conditional GAN with image-to-image translation and predict what changes when one assumption changes
  • Verify the idea with a normal case and an important edge, failure, or scale case

Explanation

A conditional GAN (cGAN) feeds extra information (a class label, a text description, or even another image) to BOTH the generator and discriminator, letting you control what gets generated rather than getting a random sample from the whole learned distribution.

Key concepts

conditional GANimage-to-image translationcontrollable generation

Example

# unconditional GAN: noise -> generator -> a random realistic image
# conditional GAN: (noise, class_label='cat') -> generator -> a realistic CAT image specifically
# image-to-image: sketch -> generator -> photorealistic image matching that sketch
Common mistake: Forgetting to feed the SAME conditioning information to the discriminator as the generator — the discriminator needs it too, to judge whether the output is realistic AND correctly matches.

10Fine-tuning & LoRA for generative models

  • Connect fine-tuning with LoRA and predict what changes when one assumption changes
  • Verify the idea with a normal case and an important edge, failure, or scale case

Explanation

Fine-tuning a pretrained GAN on a new, smaller dataset adapts it to a specific domain without training from scratch.

Key concepts

fine-tuningLoRAlow-rank adaptationfrozen weights

Example

# full fine-tuning: update ALL of a large pretrained model's weights (expensive)
# LoRA: freeze the original weights, learn small low-rank matrices
# that get added to the frozen weights -- far fewer trainable parameters
Common mistake: Assuming LoRA changes what a model can ultimately learn -- it primarily changes the efficiency/cost of adaptation, not the fundamental modeling capability.
